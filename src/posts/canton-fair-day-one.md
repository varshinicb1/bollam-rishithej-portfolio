Today was the first day of the **140th Canton Fair** in Guangzhou, and I spent it in Phase 1: electronics, machinery, hardware and industrial components. I am here with a group from the Telugu states, and I wrote down [why I came](/blog/why-i-am-going-to-guangzhou) before leaving.

These are first impressions, not conclusions. Fuller notes after the phase closes.

## The humanoid that is really a catalogue

The photograph at the top is the booth that stopped me first. A humanoid robot with its arm raised, the kind of machine that usually stands in for "the future" in a keynote slide.

What I noticed was the sign behind it. The headline is not the robot. It is **lightweight joint modules**, described as core hardware, with the modules themselves laid out on the counter like any other component.

That is the whole point of a fair like this, in one booth. The robot is the demonstration; the joint is the product. Someone has already done the hard, unglamorous work of turning an actuator into a part you can order. A team building a robot no longer has to start there.

I wrote before coming that half of what makes hardware impossible is believing you have to build something that someone already makes. That was an abstract sentence two weeks ago. Today it was a row of parts on a counter.

## Robotics, at every size

![Bollam Rishithej holding a small white companion robot with large green eyes at the Canton Fair in Guangzhou](/assets/bollam-rishithej-canton-fair-2026-companion-robot.jpg "A companion robot small enough to hold in one hand. Canton Fair Phase 1, 2026.")

At the other end of the hall, robots small enough to hold. This one has large, expressive eyes and fits in one hand.

Between the two sat everything in the middle: grippers, arms and the parts behind them. The range matters more than any single product. It means the parts of robotics that once needed a research lab are now sold the way fasteners are: by catalogue, in volume, with a lead time.

## Automation you can drink

![Bollam Rishithej holding a latte with his own face printed in the foam at the Canton Fair](/assets/bollam-rishithej-canton-fair-2026-latte-portrait.jpg "A machine printed my face onto the foam. Canton Fair, day one.")

And then a coffee printer put my face on a latte.

It is a gimmick, and a good one. It is also the most honest demonstration in the building: automation that does one small thing reliably, thousands of times a day, cheaply enough that it is given away. Most useful automation looks like this. Not dramatic, just dependable and inexpensive.

## What I am taking from day one

- **The interesting products are the parts.** Finished machines draw the crowd; components are what make other people's products possible.
- **Scale is visible here in a way it is not on a website.** You see how many companies make the same category, which tells you how mature it is.
- **Good automation is boring.** The coffee printer and the joint module have that in common.

I came to learn what is already manufacturable. On day one the answer was: more than I assumed.
